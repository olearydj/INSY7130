# HW3A: Clustering, Purpose, and Evidence

Ulrike von Luxburg, Robert C. Williamson, and Isabelle Guyon ask how clustering should be evaluated when people use it for different purposes. Their paper, [“Clustering: Science or Art?” (2012)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v27/luxburg12a.html), argues that a judgment of clustering quality needs to account for what someone intends to do with the groups.

## Why this Paper?

In this course, finding a pattern begins an investigation. A method can return neighbors, a map, or groups, but we still have to ask what those results represent, whether they hold up, and whether they help answer a question someone cares about. Clustering makes that challenge especially visible: an algorithm can produce groups even when their purpose or meaning is unsettled.

## Instructions

Write a 300 to 600 word reflection on the paper, as it relates to this course. The aim is to form a defensible position, including where you agree and where you think the paper may need qualification(s).

The assigned reading primarily relates to clustering but is supported by our work in unit 1 and the recurring themes in both. The best submissions will take that all into consideration, along with related personal experience from other courses, research, and professional experience.
